If you have seen Paraguay described as a “golden visa”, the useful question is what you intend to do with your capital. Someone opening a workshop and hiring staff in Asunción has a different immigration option from a freelancer who wants to live there and keep their existing clients. Both can look at residence. Only the first person in this example has described a local investment project.

## How much do you have to invest?

Paraguay's Ministry of Industry and Commerce (MIC) recognises four investment categories. These figures are minimum commitments in US dollars, or the equivalent in guaraníes at the official exchange rate. You still need to budget separately for the residence application, document preparation and the costs of running or holding the investment. [MIC overview of the four routes](https://www.mic.gov.py/paraguay-fortalece-la-atraccion-de-inversiones-extranjeras-con-el-nuevo-investor-pass/).

| Investment | Minimum capital | What you agree to do |
|---|---:|---|
| Industry, commerce or services | US$70,000 | Submit a business plan, create at least five formal jobs and report project progress every six months. |
| Tourism | US$150,000 | Submit a business plan for a tourism activity and report on implementation every six months. |
| Financial instruments | US$200,000 | Document the qualifying investment through an authorised institution, maintain it for at least two years and report annually. |
| Economic-use real estate | US$200,000 | Document qualifying property acquisition, development or exploitation for economic benefit. Exclusively personal or family use is excluded. The category has no business-plan or job-creation requirement. |

In the productive category, look at what is actually eligible before relying on your budget total. The annex gives equipment and machinery used by the business as examples of investment, while excluding recurring costs such as rent and salaries. If your business plan shows US$40,000 of qualifying equipment and US$35,000 in wages and rent, the US$75,000 headline budget does not clear the US$70,000 capital test on those figures. This is a planning example. The authority assesses the project you submit. [Resolution 0283/2026, Annex I, articles 3 to 6](https://www.mic.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Res.-N-0283.2026_Constancia-de-Inversionista.pdf).

## What does the Investor Pass actually give you?

There are two decisions. First, MIC's business one-stop office (SUACE) assesses the individual investor and issues a *Constancia de Inversionista Extranjero* if the requirements are met. You use that certificate to ask Migraciones for permanent residence under the investment exception to the usual temporary-first route. Approval gives you permanent immigration status. The card is renewable every ten years. Citizenship involves a [separate judicial process](/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-vs-citizenship).

A common misunderstanding concerns the five-working-day rule. Article 4 of the MIC resolution gives SUACE that period to issue the **investor certificate** after receiving the file. A request for missing information or corrections pauses the clock. The rule gives you no fixed decision date for your later residence application. [MIC resolution, articles 1 to 4](https://www.mic.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Res.-N-0283.2026_Constancia-de-Inversionista.pdf). [Migraciones’ investor application](https://migraciones.gov.py/residencia-permanente-para-inversionistas-extranjeros-suace/).

## What should you check before committing money?

1. **Describe the investment in one paragraph.** Identify who will own it, where the capital comes from, exactly how much will be invested and what evidence you will hold. An investment described as “property in Paraguay” could be a buy-to-let apartment, land for productive use or your family home. Those facts change the answer.
2. **Check the evidence with SUACE.** For financial instruments, check the issuing institution and documentary proof of the term and value. For property, have the title, registration and transaction documents matched to the annex before signing. The annex sets age limits for evidence supplied in both categories.
3. **Prepare the personal file as well.** The certificate procedure includes identity and entry records, background evidence and a sworn source-of-funds declaration. Migraciones publishes its own list covering, among other things, the certificate, birth record, proof of entry, applicable visa and clearances. A broker's receipt cannot complete either application by itself. [MIC requirements](https://www.mic.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Res.-N-0283.2026_Constancia-de-Inversionista.pdf). [Migraciones’ checklist and fees](https://migraciones.gov.py/residencia-permanente-para-inversionistas-extranjeros-suace/).

## Which route makes sense for a remote worker?

If your goal is to live in Paraguay while continuing an existing online business, the ordinary route lets you apply for temporary residence without meeting an Investor Pass capital threshold. Later you can apply for [permanent residence with evidence of your income](/blog/posts/paraguay-permanent-residency). Start with the [ordinary residence requirements](/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-requirements) if that describes you.

If you already intend to make a qualifying investment, take the project and ownership details to SUACE before moving funds. Its [investor certificate service page](https://www.mic.gov.py/constancia-de-inversionista-extranjero-suace/) identifies the responsible office and describes in-person filing. Its checklist focuses on operating businesses, so use the April resolution above for the criteria for property, financial instruments and tourism. If your real question concerns where you will owe tax on your income, examine that separately from the investment approval.
