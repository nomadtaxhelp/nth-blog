Suppose you form a Delaware LLC in November 2026 while living in Paraguay. You fund it yourself and your first customer will pay in January. It is tempting to hand the accountant a blank sales report for 2026. The two documents they need first are the formation record and the statement showing how the money got into the account.

## Who can use this filing calendar?

The example assumes a US domestic LLC with a calendar tax year, one individual owner who is a nonresident alien for US tax purposes, and no election to be taxed as a corporation. The LLC is disregarded for federal income tax but treated as a separate reporting entity for specific information-reporting purposes. A US citizen living abroad, a partnership and an LLC that elected corporate treatment each need a different starting point. [IRS guidance on single-member LLCs](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/single-member-limited-liability-companies)

If you have yet to choose an entity, start with [how Paraguay residency and a US LLC fit together](/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-us-llc). Before budgeting for a formation, ask who will prepare the first return, what records they need and what the quoted fee covers.

## Two transfers that matter before you have any sales

| Illustration | What the books should show | Why the preparer needs it |
|---|---|---|
| On 10 November the owner wires $2,000 from a personal account to fund the LLC. | Keep the transfer confirmation, amount, date and evidence of the capital contribution. | An owner contribution falls within the IRS definition for this entity. Zero sales does not remove it. |
| On 18 December the LLC sends $500 back to the same owner as a distribution. | Record the distribution separately and retain the outgoing statement. | It is a second reportable movement. A year-end net balance of $1,500 conceals the two transactions. |

The example identifies the payments as capital and a distribution because their purpose is known. Money sent to an owner might instead reimburse a documented business expense or repay a loan. Keep the invoice or agreement so the preparer can classify what happened. They should also check formation expenses you paid personally and transactions with any other related party. The IRS generally calls for a separate Form 5472 for each related party with whom the reporting entity had reportable transactions. [IRS instructions, including Part V](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)

## The first filing calendar

| When in this example | What to do |
|---|---|
| November 2026, at formation | Save the accepted formation document, ownership record, EIN confirmation and any tax election. Record who paid the formation and agent fees. |
| At 31 December 2026 | Reconcile the statements to the books. Give the preparer the two gross transfers, receipts for expenses paid personally, and details of any other related parties. |
| By 15 April 2027 under the normal calendar-year rule | For these transactions, attach Form 5472 to the abbreviated, or *pro forma*, Form 1120. If more time is needed, submit the applicable Form 7004 extension by the original deadline and confirm the extended due date with the preparer. |
| By 1 June 2027 for this Delaware example | Check and pay the Delaware LLC annual tax for 2026. The state has a separate payment process. An IRS extension does not complete the state step. |
| After filing | Keep copies of what was filed, proof of dispatch and state payment confirmation. Set next year's reminders and update the records if ownership or tax classification changes. |

The IRS ordinarily uses the fifteenth day of the fourth month after year end for this example, with weekend and legal-holiday adjustments. The instructions specify a dedicated fax or postal route for foreign-owned US disregarded entities and for their extensions. Use the current destination in those instructions. A generic corporate e-filing confirmation is insufficient evidence that this package was sent. Delaware’s rule belongs to this example. Other states can have different dates, fees and reporting requirements. [IRS Form 5472 filing and extension instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) · [IRS due-date instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120) · [Delaware annual tax instructions](https://corp.delaware.gov/alt-entitytaxinstructions/)

## What to ask before paying a filing service

Ask the preparer to identify the owner, entity classification, tax year and related parties they used. Then ask for the names of the federal and state forms covered by the fee. A registered-agent renewal pays for an agent. A receipt for it says nothing about whether a federal information return was prepared. The IRS initial penalty for failing to file Form 5472 as prescribed is $25,000. If a previous deadline has passed, give a US tax professional the records and any notices promptly. [IRS instructions on penalties](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)

Finally, tell the preparer where you worked, what the company sold and whether it had US staff, premises or activity. Those facts can raise additional federal or state questions. Ask your adviser in the country where you live how it treats the LLC and payments from it.
