Suppose you hand back the keys to your flat in Rotterdam in June, spend July working abroad and collect a Paraguayan residence card in October. Which date goes on your Dutch tax return? Start with the date your living arrangements actually changed. Your flight, your municipal deregistration and your residence card each document a different event.

## When does the Netherlands consider you to have emigrated?

The Belastingdienst looks at the overall pattern of your life. Where do you usually stay? Where do your partner and children live? Where do you work, visit a doctor, have health insurance or take part in associations? If you keep a home, describe who can use it and how often you return. A count of nights abroad alone leaves most of those questions unanswered. [Belastingdienst guidance on emigration](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/individuals/content/moving-abroad-emigration).

A practical way to prepare is to write a short timeline with the dates something actually changed. Include any overlap. For example, “I started renting in Asunción in May, but my partner and I still used our Utrecht home until August” gives an adviser more to work with than choosing May as the departure date because it appears on a lease.

## When and how do you deregister from the BRP?

If you will live abroad for **more than eight months within a year**, even across several trips, you must deregister as a resident. This also applies if you keep a Dutch home. The process moves your details into the non-residents register (RNI), which forms part of the BRP. Your citizen service number (BSN) remains valid. [Government.nl deregistration rule](https://www.government.nl/themes/government-and-democracy/personal-data/question-and-answer/when-should-i-deregister-from-the-personal-records-database).

1. Check the departure procedure with your municipality before making travel arrangements. The reporting window runs from five days before you leave through departure day, including weekends and public holidays. If some family members at the address stay behind, you must attend in person.
2. If you need international proof of deregistration, ask the municipality about timing. NetherlandsWorldwide says the date of deregistration and the date of emigration must match for the municipality to give you that proof.
3. Give your foreign address if you know it. If it is still unknown, you can supply the details later. Check that your DigiD works abroad and that you can receive correspondence about assessments and benefits.

Municipal procedures vary. If you already left while registered, contact the municipality where you were living. [NetherlandsWorldwide deregistration instructions](https://www.netherlandsworldwide.nl/personal-records-database-brp/how-deregister-brp) and [moving abroad checklist](https://www.netherlandsworldwide.nl/moving-abroad).

## What goes on the Dutch departure-year return?

For a required return covering a year spent partly abroad, use the part-year income tax return in **Mijn Belastingdienst**. The paper alternative is **form M** for the relevant year. If you are an entrepreneur filing on paper, the Belastingdienst also requires the M annual report. Follow the instructions and deadline for your own filing year. [Official filing instructions](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/individuals/content/partly-living-outside-the-netherlands-m-tax-return).

Before filing, assemble your salary statements or business accounts, the dates and places you performed work, details of any Dutch property and the papers that support your move. Separate what you received before and after the proposed departure date. That gives the preparer a way to check each payment against your status and the applicable rules. The location of the bank account receiving it settles neither question.

## What if you keep Dutch property, a BV or a pension?

| What you keep | What to establish before filing |
|---|---|
| A home in the Netherlands | Who used it, whether it remained available to you, and how it should be reported after emigration. Dutch real estate can remain within Dutch taxation for non-residents. |
| Dutch work or business income | Where you carried out the work, what the payment was for and which country can tax it. Keep a breakdown of workdays and contracts. |
| A substantial shareholding, such as an interest in your BV | Whether the departure gives rise to a protective assessment. Ask separately about the company, payroll and any planned share sale or distribution. |
| Dutch pension or annuity rights | Whether a protective assessment applies, what events could trigger collection, and whether moving outside the EU creates a security requirement in your case. |

A protective assessment can record tax that becomes payable later under specified conditions. If you receive one, retain it separately from the ordinary income tax bill, record any deferral conditions and ask about a planned transaction before carrying it out. [Belastingdienst on non-resident income and assets](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/individuals/content/deductions-when-living-abroad-with-income-from-the-netherlands) and [protective assessments](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/individuals/content/protective-assessment-in-the-case-of-emigration).

## Two departures that look similar on paper

**A freelancer gives up her Dutch home.** In June she hands the keys back, moves with her partner and starts performing her contracts abroad. She retains a Dutch client who pays into her existing bank account. Her adviser can match her handover, foreign lease, family move and work records to the proposed date. The client relationship still deserves review, but the client's address alone does not locate her working day.

**A founder spends the same months abroad.** His family stays in Utrecht, he returns to their home between trips and he runs a Dutch BV. He also has a Paraguay residence permit. His adviser needs the dates each home was available, where he worked and made business decisions, when his family intends to move, and how the BV pays him. If the two cases have the same flight dates, their answers can still differ.

If the timeline supports more than one reading, the Belastingdienst invites you to write to your tax office setting out the circumstances.
