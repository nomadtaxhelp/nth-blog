Digital nomads often discover Paraguay through the promise of paying 0% tax. There is a real reason for the interest: Paraguay’s personal income tax focuses on Paraguayan-source income. The useful question is **which of your income streams fall outside that scope**, and what records support the answer.

This guide explains that distinction for remote workers and travelling founders. It concerns Paraguay’s personal income tax, IRP. Company tax, VAT and another country’s rules require their own assessment.

## What makes income foreign-source?

Article 48 of Paraguay’s Tax Law 6380/2019 looks at activities carried out in Paraguay, property situated there and rights economically used there. For a service business, start with where the work happens. The location of the bank receiving the payment is a separate issue.

DNIT’s published consultation 829 gives a practical example. It considers IT and consulting services performed outside Paraguay for foreign clients. Its analysis distinguishes work done abroad from work done in Paraguay. In the mixed scenario, the portion performed in Paraguay is treated differently from the portion performed abroad. Read the [official DNIT consultation](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/20123/422349/AFECTACI%C3%93N%2BDEL%2BIRP%2BPOR%2BASESOR%C3%8DA%2BPRESTADO%2BFUERA%2BDEL%2BPARAGUAY.%2B%2BN%C2%B0%2B829%2BCV.pdf/9e2c3311-8fd6-0711-db26-fc4aa517784a?t=1770985417003).

That consultation addresses the facts presented to DNIT. It is useful evidence for understanding the rule, rather than a universal exemption for everyone calling themselves a digital nomad.

## Three remote-work examples

These are illustrations of the questions to assess, not individual tax rulings.

| Situation | What to examine |
|---|---|
| You perform consulting work outside Paraguay and invoice foreign clients | Document the work location and the applicable source rules. The country where you actually work may also have rules to consider. |
| You live and perform the same consulting work in Asunción | The foreign client does not remove the Paraguayan work location. Assess the service as work performed in Paraguay. |
| You split the work between Paraguay and other countries | Keep records that allow the work and income to be attributed properly. A single overseas bank account does not resolve the split. |

The difference is the activity behind the payment. Two people can invoice the same customer and receive money in the same currency yet need different tax treatment.

## Separate salary, business income and investments

Avoid putting all income into one “online income” category. Prepare a simple list before changing your structure:

- **Employment:** employer, duties, payroll arrangement and where you work.
- **Freelance or consulting income:** contracts, work locations and invoices.
- **Company profits and distributions:** the company’s jurisdiction, activities, management and how it pays you.
- **Investments and property:** the asset, income type and countries involved.

A US LLC can be part of an appropriate business setup, but it does not erase the underlying activity or reporting duties. Our [Paraguay and US LLC guide](/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-us-llc) explains the company side separately.

## Does a residency card establish the tax result?

The residence permit gives you immigration status. The cédula identifies you. The RUC registers you for applicable tax obligations, and a fiscal residence certificate is a further document with its own requirements. None is a substitute for classifying your income correctly.

See the [residency, RUC and tax-certificate guide](/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-ruc-tax-certificate) for what each document does. If you are still choosing the route, begin with our [Paraguay tax residency guide](/blog/posts/paraguay-tax-residency-guide).

## What should you prepare for a workable setup?

Keep your contracts, invoices, travel history and evidence of where the work took place. Map your intended travel and business arrangements before opening new accounts or moving payments. Align your departure from your previous country with the new setup, using the relevant country’s rules.

US citizens and green card holders need a separate US tax analysis because the US system does not operate like Paraguay’s territorial personal tax system. The same Paraguay result cannot simply be applied to every nationality and existing obligation.
