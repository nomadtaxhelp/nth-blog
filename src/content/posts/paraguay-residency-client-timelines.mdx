If you are booking time off work for a residency trip to Asunción, you have two decisions to make. How many nights should you reserve for appointments? And when can you reasonably expect to have a document you can show a bank or another authority? The dates in our own files illustrate why those questions need separate answers.

On 26 November 2026 we reviewed all nine cases marked post-visit in our service records. Five had a date recorded for receipt of the temporary residence card. Three of those five also had an arrival date. We could corroborate the visit and its timing closely enough to describe two of them below. We have left out personal details and rounded neither interval.

## What happened in two completed visits?

| | What happened in Asunción | What we could verify afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| One client spent four nights in Paraguay. | The itinerary and the completion note agree on the dates of the visit. The file does not identify the exact day the application was lodged. | Our team recorded having the temporary residence document in hand **70 calendar days after arrival**. That interval starts on the day the client arrived. |
| Another client filed during the first visit. | The completion note records the application, fingerprints and photo on the same date. The file does not establish the total length of the stay. | The first update we found saying that the card was ready but uncollected came **60 days after filing**. The team recorded receiving the card **100 days after filing**. |

In the second case, someone checking progress at day 60 and someone checking again at day 100 would have received different answers. The card was already reported ready at the first check. The intervening updates dealt with collection and the authority to collect it. Counting all 100 days as government processing time would assign those last 40 days to the wrong part of the process. The date the card reached the client personally is a further question.

## How much time should you set aside in Asunción?

The four-night stay above is one completed itinerary. A different perspective comes from a client who [described her own visit on video](https://nomadtaxhelp.com/reviews/julia). She allowed two weeks in Paraguay and remembers her local steps taking about a day. That tells you how the appointments fitted inside *her* trip. The video does not give a filing-to-approval date.

None of the nine post-visit files had a completed departure field in the client portal. We could recover the four-night stay because a separate note confirmed the visit dates. An outbound flight booked in advance would give us only a plan. There is too little comparable evidence to give an average stay.

Before paying for a return flight, ask which office appointments have been arranged, which documents have already been checked and what would move an appointment to a later day. Our [visit-planning guide](https://nomadtaxhelp.com/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-days-and-timeline) sets out the current allowance for the first trip.

## Which document issues caused a delay?

One post-visit file records a pre-travel hold while the team waited for an apostilled criminal-record certificate. A later update confirms that the authenticated paperwork reached the team. In that case we can identify what was holding up preparation. We cannot trace a second flight to the missing apostille. Across the nine files, the records do not consistently say whether a document problem caused another visit, so we cannot give a frequency or a list of the most common causes.

A useful question to send before you travel is “Have you checked the issued certificate and its authentication, or are you still waiting to see one of them?” An order confirmation for a certificate answers a different question from a review of the document you will actually present. Use the [document checklist](https://nomadtaxhelp.com/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-requirements) to prepare for that conversation. Migraciones publishes the requirements for the ordinary [temporary residence application](https://migraciones.gov.py/residencia-temporal/).

## What should you ask for after filing?

Ask for the filing date and the application reference. When your next update comes in, ask which event it confirms: a decision was issued, a card can be collected, someone has collected it, or the document has reached you. If a representative is collecting it, establish in advance who is authorised and how you will receive it.

Ask which document the update refers to as well. The temporary residence card and the Paraguayan identity card, or cédula, have separate milestones. Five of our nine reviewed files had a temporary-card receipt date and three had a cédula receipt date. An empty date field does not establish what the client held on the day we reviewed the records. Migraciones grants temporary residence, while the identity card is issued by the Policía Nacional’s Departamento de Identificaciones. [Official explanation](https://migraciones.gov.py/residencia-temporal/).

## How we checked the cases

We selected the nine post-visit files before looking at their durations. We checked the dates against available completion notes and dated updates from the team in Paraguay. We excluded one apparent arrival-to-receipt pair from the illustrations because the sources did not agree on the arrival day. None of the nine files contained a complete pair of verified filing and official decision dates that we could use to report a government processing time. The two examples have different starting events, so averaging them would obscure what each one measures.

If you are comparing providers, ask each one who checks the documents before travel, who accompanies you in Asunción and who follows up on collection after you leave. You can see how we divide those responsibilities on our [Paraguay residency service page](https://nomadtaxhelp.com/services).
