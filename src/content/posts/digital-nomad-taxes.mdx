Being able to work from anywhere gives you more choices about where to base your life. It can also give you a legitimate way to keep more of what you earn. The useful question is which setup works for your income and the way you want to travel.

This guide is for digital nomads, remote workers and travelling founders comparing their options. We coordinate Paraguay setups at Nomad Tax Help, so that is the example we explain in most depth. The rules below come from the linked tax authorities; the planning framework is our practical way of bringing those questions together.

## Do digital nomads pay tax?

Yes, digital nomads can owe tax. Working online does not create a separate worldwide tax category. Start by distinguishing four things that often get bundled together:

| Part of your setup | What it answers | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Residence permit | Whether you can live in a country under that immigration route | Your complete tax position |
| Personal tax residence | Which country's residence rules you meet | Whether every kind of income is taxed there |
| Source of income | Where income is treated as arising under the relevant law | Where your bank account happens to be |
| Business structure | Who earns the revenue and has business obligations | How every payment to you is taxed personally |

Think of these as four connected decisions. A residence card, a company certificate or a foreign bank account cannot answer all four on its own.

## How can a digital nomad legally pay 0% tax?

A zero income-tax result needs a complete set of circumstances: the income qualifies for the treatment in your chosen base, your previous residence obligations have been addressed, and the countries connected to your work or business do not impose another charge on it. Different income streams can produce different answers for the same person.

Paraguay provides a useful example. In consultation 829, DNIT considered IT and consultancy services performed outside Paraguay for foreign clients. It concluded that the services described were outside Paraguayan personal income tax because they were not performed there. That is a specific source-of-income analysis, rather than an exemption for everybody with an overseas customer. [Read DNIT's published consultation](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/20123/422349/AFECTACI%C3%93N%2BDEL%2BIRP%2BPOR%2BASESOR%C3%8DA%2BPRESTADO%2BFUERA%2BDEL%2BPARAGUAY.%2B%2BN%C2%B0%2B829%2BCV.pdf/9e2c3311-8fd6-0711-db26-fc4aa517784a?t=1770985417003).

For a travelling contractor, that distinction matters. Work performed abroad and work performed from an apartment in Paraguay need separate analysis. The country where you perform the work may also have its own rules. The Paraguay result alone is not a calculation of your total worldwide tax.

## Where do you pay tax if you keep travelling?

Build the answer around your actual homes, days and work. Staying below 183 days in each country is not a universal exemption.

For example, HMRC considers automatic UK and overseas tests and sufficient ties. Homes, family and work can affect the result alongside days. UK residents normally pay UK tax on worldwide income; non-residents generally remain liable on UK income. [HMRC's residence guidance](https://www.gov.uk/tax-foreign-income/residence).

Treat departure as a practical project: establish the relevant tax year, map the residence tests, record the date and circumstances of your move, and identify any continuing local income. You then have a clear brief for a home-country adviser rather than a vague question about whether travelling is enough. Our [home-country exit guide](/blog/posts/european-nomad-tax-playbook) provides that checklist.

US citizens and resident aliens need a different calculation. The IRS generally taxes worldwide income even when they live abroad, although exclusions and credits can reduce a qualifying liability. A Paraguay residence card does not replace those rules. [IRS foreign earned income guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion).

## Your income type changes the decision

Prepare a separate line for each way you earn money. This makes the next conversation much more useful.

| You receive | Bring to the review |
|---|---|
| Salary | Employer location, employment contract and where you physically work |
| Freelance or consulting fees | Services delivered, client countries, work locations and contracts |
| Company profits or distributions | Incorporation, ownership, management and how you pay yourself |
| Investment income | Asset type, country of origin and any tax already withheld |
| Property income | Property location, ownership and expenses |

Ask for income tax, company tax, payroll or social contributions, VAT where relevant, and filing duties to be considered separately. A low personal rate does not describe the whole cost of a business.

## Choose a tax base you can actually maintain

Write down your intended travel year before choosing a country. How many months would you enjoy spending there? Do you want a permanent home? Will you work locally? Are you keeping your current company?

Those answers make the comparison meaningful. A destination that works for someone living there most of the year may be a poor fit for someone who wants several bases. Our [comparison of Paraguay, the UAE, Georgia and Cyprus](/blog/posts/best-tax-residency-digital-nomads) covers the different residence commitments and income questions.

For Paraguay, distinguish the initial appointment trip from document processing and ongoing requirements. Migraciones describes ordinary temporary residence as valid for up to two years and a prerequisite to the ordinary permanent route. [Migraciones requirements](https://migraciones.gov.py/residencia-temporal/). Our [days and timeline guide](/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-days-and-timeline) explains how to plan the stages.

## Compare what you keep after all costs

Use your own numbers rather than somebody else's headline saving:

**Expected annual benefit = current tax and compliance cost − expected new tax and compliance cost − extra annual costs of maintaining the setup.**

Then account for one-off setup and moving costs separately. Include travel you would not otherwise take, required accommodation, documents, renewals and professional support. This is a planning calculation, not a forecast of a particular tax result.

For example, if an illustrative annual saving after ongoing costs is $12,000 and the one-off cost is $6,000, simple payback is six months. If that annual saving is $3,000, payback is two years. The same setup can be attractive to one person and unnecessary for another.

The [Paraguay setup cost guide](/blog/posts/paraguay-tax-residency-cost-2026) shows what to include in a real quote. Compare like-for-like scope rather than a government fee against a complete service.

## A sensible order to get started

1. List your income streams, company structure and current tax residence.
2. Map the next year's travel and where you expect to work.
3. Shortlist bases that fit that life and check their official requirements.
4. Coordinate the departure, destination and company questions before changing the structure.
5. Keep the evidence and put renewals and filings in a calendar.
