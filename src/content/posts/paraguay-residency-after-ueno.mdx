## What the Ueno news means for your Paraguay plan

If you are a digital nomad, remote worker or travelling founder considering Paraguay, the Ueno news raises a reasonable question: where should your money actually live?

The official communication published by Paraguay's judiciary confirms that the central bank ordered a resolution process for Ueno Bank under a decision dated 2 November 2026. It concerns a bank, rather than the immigration system that grants residency. [Read the official communication](https://www.pj.gov.py/notas/30918-corte-suprema-de-justicia-socializa-comunicacion-de-la-superintendencia-de-bancos-sobre-ueno-bank-sa).

For someone planning a new setup, the practical lesson is to choose residency and banking deliberately. You do not need to use a Paraguayan bank as the centre of your international finances simply because you have Paraguayan residency. Our approach is an overseas banking structure, with accounts selected for the jobs they need to do.

Felix's video above explains that approach. The provider examples in the recording are starting points to investigate, rather than promises that every applicant will qualify.

## Two account roles: earning and spending

The starting point is straightforward: separate the money belonging to your company from the money you use personally.

| Part of the setup | What it does | What needs to fit |
|---|---|---|
| Overseas business account | Receives company invoices and pays business expenses | Your company, activity, owners, operating address and payment routes |
| Personal spending account | Receives properly recorded personal transfers and supports everyday spending | Your actual residence, identity documents, currencies and card needs |
| A second access route | Gives you another way to receive or spend money if one account is unavailable | Genuine eligibility, ongoing access and sensible balances |

For example, your company receives a client payment into its business account. Business costs are paid there. Money distributed to you personally is recorded appropriately and transferred to your personal account for travel and living expenses.

That separation makes the flow easier to manage. It does not, by itself, determine how the income is taxed. The company, the work you do and the countries involved still need to fit the wider plan.

## Overseas business banking with a US company

Felix discusses US company banking in the video because a suitable US company can open up useful payment routes for international businesses. It is an option to assess against your activity, rather than a company everybody needs to form.

Mercury, for example, publishes requirements for a US-registered company with existing or planned US operations. It also checks the company and founders, physical operating address, residential addresses and other eligibility conditions. International founders can apply, but incorporation alone does not secure approval. [Mercury's requirements](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28770467511060-Eligibility-and-requirements-for-opening-a-Mercury-account).

Before choosing an account, work through how you will actually use it: client invoices, payment-processor payouts, supplier payments, currencies and transfers to yourself. Prepare documents that accurately explain that activity. See our [Paraguay residency and US LLC guide](/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-us-llc) for the company and filing side.

## Personal accounts, Wise and Revolut

A provider that accepts your company may have different rules for a personal account. Your nationality, immigration residence, actual residential address and tax residence are also different pieces of information.

Wise's published list of places where customers can live and hold money does not currently include Paraguay. That is relevant to personal-account planning; it should not be turned into a blanket claim that every business with a Paraguay-resident owner is prohibited. [Wise's supported-residence list](https://wise.com/help/articles/2813542/where-do-i-need-to-live-to-hold-money-with-wise).

For a US business, Wise checks registration, the real trading address and owners' residential information. A registered-agent or mail-forwarding address is not a substitute for the real trading address. Confirm your particular company and ownership circumstances before relying on the account. [Wise Business verification](https://wise.com/help/articles/2953878/how-do-i-verify-my-us-business).

Revolut's published personal-account sign-up countries also do not currently list Paraguay. Check the provider's current rules and the implications of any change of residence for an existing account. [Revolut's supported countries](https://help.revolut.com/help/profile-and-plan/profile-plan/verifying-identity/what-countries-are-supported/).

The practical aim is an account that supports your real circumstances, with the currencies, transfer routes and spending access you need. Our [banking documents and account requirements guide](/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-banking) gives you a preparation checklist.

## What deposit protection actually covers

The video describes some overseas accounts as fully deposit-insured. The precise coverage needs checking for the account you choose.

In the US, the standard FDIC limit is **$250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category**. Higher advertised protection can involve funds spread across multiple banks, with conditions that must be met. It is not unlimited protection simply because an account is American. [FDIC insurance limits](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/faq).

For an app or fintech, identify the underlying bank and the requirements for pass-through insurance. Deposit insurance against a bank failure is different from protection against the failure of the app or nonbank company. Investments and crypto should not be treated as insured bank deposits. [FDIC guidance on third-party apps](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2024-06/banking-third-party-apps).

That is why we look at the whole arrangement: where funds are held, what protection applies and how you would access your money if one provider were unavailable.
