You fly out on 1 October. You start full-time overseas work on 7 October. Your last UK home ceases to be available to you on 31 October. Which date goes on the tax return? It depends on the case you qualify for. Two people on the same flight can have different dates, and one may qualify for no split at all.

## Establish the whole-year result first

The UK tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April. Determine your UK residence for the entire year under the Statutory Residence Test. The three departure cases apply to people who are UK resident in the departure year and were UK resident in the preceding year. Each also has a condition for the following year. Where the conditions are met, the split arises under the rules. It can affect which income and gains fall within UK tax in the overseas part. UK-source charges may continue. [HMRC’s Statutory Residence Test guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rdr3-statutory-residence-test-srt/guidance-note-for-statutory-residence-test-srt-rdr3).

## Which of the three departure cases could apply?

| Case | What changes during the year | What to check before relying on it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. You start full-time work abroad | There is a qualifying period of overseas work that runs to the end of the UK tax year. | Check sufficient overseas hours, significant breaks, UK days and UK workdays against the limits for that period. Next year you must meet the full-time work abroad automatic overseas test. |
| 2. You move to live overseas with a working partner | Your partner meets Case 1 this year or met it in the previous year, and you move to continue living together. | Check prior cohabitation, homes, where you actually live and permitted UK days after the deemed departure day. You must be non-UK resident in the following year. |
| 3. You cease to have a UK home | You had a UK home at the start of the year and have no UK home for the remainder after a particular date. | For the rest of that year you must spend fewer than 16 days in the UK. There is also a six-month condition linked to one overseas country, and you must be non-UK resident the following year. |

For Case 3, the overseas condition can be met by becoming tax resident in that country within six months, by being present there at the end of every day for six months, or by having your only home or all your homes there within six months. If you intend to travel continually, check which route your facts support. Giving up a UK tenancy alone answers only one part of the test. [HMRC’s overseas work case](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig21040). [HMRC’s accompanying partner case](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig21090). [HMRC’s overseas home conditions](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig21130).

## How to find the split date

If more than one departure case applies, HMRC puts Case 1 first, then Case 2, then Case 3. Use that order even when another case would produce an earlier date. For Case 1, identify the beginning of the qualifying overseas work period. HMRC’s worked example shows how to calculate the UK day and workday limits for a partial year. For Case 2, take the later of when you join your partner to live overseas and when their overseas part started under Case 1. For Case 3, the overseas part starts on the date you cease to have a UK home. [HMRC’s split-year priority rules](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig21030). [HMRC’s worked example for overseas work](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig21080). [HMRC’s Case 2 date rule](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig21110). [HMRC’s worked example for giving up a UK home](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig21140).

### Two return visits that change the answer

Imagine someone meets the residence conditions for Case 3, has given up their only UK home and established their only home abroad within six months. They are overseas throughout the rest of the departure year apart from two UK visits. The first adds seven countable UK days and the second adds eight. The total of 15 fits the Case 3 limit. Extending the second trip by one countable day makes it 16 and the case fails. The test uses actual countable days, so check the itinerary against HMRC's day-counting rules before drawing a conclusion. [HMRC’s overseas home conditions](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig21130). [HMRC’s Statutory Residence Test guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rdr3-statutory-residence-test-srt/guidance-note-for-statutory-residence-test-srt-rdr3).

Now change the facts. Someone leaves to start a job abroad and retains a UK home. Retaining that home rules out Case 3 while it remains a home, but it does not by itself settle Case 1. Their timesheets, UK workdays, breaks and the following year's working pattern become central. A UK visit with four hours of work may count towards both a day limit and a workday limit. A quick visit can matter even when the annual total looks low. [HMRC’s overseas work case](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig21040). [HMRC’s worked example for overseas work](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/residence-and-fig-regime-manual/rfig21080).

## What to give the person completing your return

Bring the prior year's residence result, your departure-year day log, start date and hours of overseas work, home dates, details of both partners where relevant, and the following year's travel and work records. If a dividend, sale or bonus fell close to the potential boundary, give the adviser the actual paperwork and dates. They can check the charging rules for that item as well as the residence position. If next year's facts change after filing, ask promptly whether the departure return needs correcting.
