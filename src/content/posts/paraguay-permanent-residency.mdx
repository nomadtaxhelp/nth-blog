Look at the expiry date printed on your temporary residence card. That date determines when you can apply for permanent residence under Paraguay's ordinary immigration route. The date of your first flight, the date you collected your cédula and the date you opened a tax account can all be different.

## What happens if your temporary card is close to expiry?

| Time relative to the expiry date | What Migraciones allows |
|---|---|
| In the three months before expiry | Apply for a change of category to permanent residence. |
| Up to one month after expiry | You may still apply for the change of category, with a late fine. |
| More than one month and up to six months after expiry | The published late procedure is an extension of temporary residence for an equivalent period, with a fine. |
| More than six months after expiry | The published extension window has closed. Contact Migraciones with your dates and immigration history to establish your options. |

Read the [official category-change procedure](https://migraciones.gov.py/residencia-permanente-para-el-cambio-de-categoria-de-residente-temporal/) for the precise dates and fees before arranging travel. If you entered under the Mercosur agreement, use that route’s procedure for filing deadlines. Resolution 407 aligns the economic evidence across both regimes.

## Which proof of income fits your situation?

Migraciones changed the rules for permanent applications lodged from 6 July 2026. Someone on Paraguayan payroll, someone invoicing clients locally and someone employed remotely can all have regular income and need different documents. Three of the twelve categories are particularly relevant to our readers.

| Your circumstances | Evidence in the official annex |
|---|---|
| Employed under the local employee category | Proof of enrolment with the Instituto de Previsión Social (IPS), or an employment contract endorsed by the labour ministry and duly approved and registered. |
| Self-employed in trade or services | Individual taxpayer registration, the tax compliance certificate, and either the last three months’ VAT returns or the last year’s personal income tax (IRP) return. The records must show activity. |
| Working remotely | A work certificate or letter establishing the relationship, what you earn and how your pay or fees reach you. Foreign documents need the required legalisation or apostille and Spanish translation. |

A British developer employed by a London company, for example, could ask HR for a letter stating the employment relationship, income and payment method, then confirm how to authenticate it. A self-employed designer who invoices in Paraguay would instead check that their registration and returns show the activity they describe. A contractor billing overseas clients through a company should give Migraciones the real contracts and payment arrangement and ask which evidence fits. A registration number by itself tells the officer little about how the person earns a living.

The annex also covers pensioners, dependants, company owners and other circumstances. A pension certificate, for example, must indicate both the amount and how it is received. Read the category that describes your actual circumstances, especially if your work has changed since your temporary application. [Resolution 407/2026](https://migraciones.gov.py/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/RESOLUCION-DNM-Nro-407-28.05.2026-ACREDITACION-DE-SOLVENCIA-ECONOMICA-PARA-RESIDENCIA-PERMANENTE-1.pdf). [Migraciones’ published annex and checklist](https://migraciones.gov.py/residencia-permanente-para-el-cambio-de-categoria-de-residente-temporal/).

## What else belongs in your file?

Have your current home-country identity document, Paraguayan cédula and temporary residence card available. The checklist gives a lost-document alternative for the two Paraguayan cards and asks for supporting records when details such as your name, marriage or occupation have changed. From age 14 it also lists four separate background records, issued by police informatics, police identification, the judiciary and INTERPOL. Check the originals, certified copies and authentication rules against the [current application list](https://migraciones.gov.py/residencia-permanente-para-el-cambio-de-categoria-de-residente-temporal/).

Check your absences at the same time. Article 55 of the migration law identifies an unjustified absence of more than a year for a temporary resident as grounds for cancellation, subject to authorisation by Migraciones. A card with time left on it cannot answer that question. If you have spent that long abroad, take dated entry and exit records and any authorisation to Migraciones before relying on the dates in the table. [Migration Law, articles 54 and 55](https://www.bacn.gov.py/leyes-paraguayas/10973/ley-n-6984-de-migraciones-0).

## What changes once you receive approval?

Permanent residence lets you settle indefinitely. The residence card still comes up for renewal every ten years, and the law addresses unjustified absences of more than three years for permanent residents. Replacing a damaged card, renewing an existing permanent card and changing immigration category are three different applications. Keep the decision granting permanent residence with your card. Its date also matters if you later consider [Paraguayan naturalisation](/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-vs-citizenship).
