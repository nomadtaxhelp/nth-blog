Suppose you and a friend both earn a living from overseas clients. You spend most of the year travelling between projects. Your friend wants to rent a flat in Tbilisi and work there most weeks. Georgia’s small-business tax rate might be central to your friend’s decision and almost beside the point for yours. The difference starts with where you will actually live and do the work.

## Paraguay if you want a South American base

A consultant visiting customers abroad and another delivering the same work from Asunción can have very different tax results, even if they invoice the same overseas company. In a published ruling, Paraguay’s tax authority, DNIT, examined where the consulting work took place. It treated services performed abroad for foreign clients differently from work done inside Paraguay. If you move your working days, revisit the tax analysis even when your client list and payment account stay exactly the same. [DNIT’s consulting-income ruling](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/20123/422349/AFECTACI%C3%93N%2BDEL%2BIRP%2BPOR%2BASESOR%C3%8DA%2BPRESTADO%2BFUERA%2BDEL%2BPARAGUAY.%2B%2BN%C2%B0%2B829%2BCV.pdf/9e2c3311-8fd6-0711-db26-fc4aa517784a?t=1770985417003).

There is also a practical question. Can you attend the immigration appointments and plan for the later application? The ordinary temporary permit lasts up to two years. The first appointment visit, later document collection and permanent residency each need their own place on the calendar. ## Dubai if you want to live or work there

An employee relocating to Dubai to keep a remote job has a different shopping list from a founder setting up a UAE business. The employee can start by checking employer permission and the appropriate immigration route. The founder also needs to understand company costs and what gets taxed. Asking both people to price the same company-formation package would distort the comparison.

Under UAE domestic rules, an individual may qualify after at least 183 days in a consecutive twelve-month period. A separate route requires at least 90 days in such a period, qualifying nationality or UAE residence permission, and either a permanent place to live or employment or business in the UAE. Another test considers the person’s usual or main home together with the centre of their financial and personal interests. Work out which test your circumstances support before pricing a visit schedule. [UAE Cabinet Resolution 85, Article 4](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1574/download).

For an individual carrying on business in the UAE, the Federal Tax Authority uses annual business turnover above AED 1 million to determine whether its corporate tax rules apply. It excludes wages and qualifying personal investment and real estate investment income from that business test. That distinction matters if you draw a salary while also selling services in your own name. [FTA guidance for individuals](https://www.tax.gov.ae/en/taxes/corporate.tax/corporate.tax.topics/basis.of.taxation.natural.person.aspx) · [Paraguay and Dubai side by side](/blog/posts/paraguay-vs-dubai-tax-residency)

## Georgia if you want to run a business there

Start with a recent invoice and your client contract. What service did you sell, who invoiced for it, and was the payment actually salary? Georgia’s 1% small-business rate applies to qualifying income of an eligible entrepreneur with small-business status. The code excludes salary from that regime and allows restrictions on activities. Under the usual threshold, the rate on qualifying income rises to 3% from the month gross economic income exceeds GEL 500,000 through the end of the year. The business test and the residence test answer different questions. The ordinary individual residence rule generally requires at least 183 days in a continuous twelve-month period ending in the tax year. [Georgian Tax Code, Articles 34 and 88–90](https://www.matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1043717).

A self-employed person expecting to work most of the year in Tbilisi has good reason to investigate. Before including 1% in your budget, get confirmation that your actual work qualifies and that you have the right to carry it out there. ## Cyprus if a European home is part of the plan

Someone who wants to keep a home in Cyprus, work there and spend part of the year travelling has a reason to look at the 60-day route. There are several conditions to meet together. The Tax Department lists at least 60 days in Cyprus, no more than 183 days in another country, an owned or rented permanent home in Cyprus and qualifying business, employment or office-holder activity there. Ending the relevant employment or business during the year can affect eligibility. Cyprus also has a separate route for stays totalling more than 183 days in the tax year. [Cyprus Tax Department’s conditions](https://www.gov.cy/mof-tax/en/documents/tax-residency-domicility/).

If someone quotes a saving based on Cyprus’s non-domicile provisions, ask which part of your income the calculation covers. The department treats income-tax residence and domicile for the special defence contribution as separate questions. A calculation about investment income tells you little about the tax due on your salary. [Cyprus Tax Department on domicile](https://www.gov.cy/mof-tax/en/documents/tax-residency-domicility/).

## How to compare your last two options

Put next year’s calendar beside a breakdown of what you earn. Show each adviser the same expected working days, employer or clients, homes and income types. Then ask for two figures in one currency, the first-year total and a normal year after that. Include the housing you will really use, flights you would otherwise skip, annual filings, company costs and estimated taxes. For a founder with a family home elsewhere, the [home-country departure review](/blog/posts/european-nomad-tax-playbook) belongs in that calculation too.

If your shortlist relies on leaving the US tax system, check that premise first. US citizens and resident aliens generally remain subject to US taxation on worldwide income, with relief available to those who qualify. [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion).
