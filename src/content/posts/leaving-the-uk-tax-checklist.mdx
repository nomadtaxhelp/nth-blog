If you leave the UK in October, your tax year still runs to the following 5 April. Before booking a flight, make a list of the things you will keep in the UK. A flat you plan to let, a job you will do partly on return visits, and a company you own each create a different question. This checklist takes you from that first list through your first tax return from abroad.

## Before you leave, put the evidence in one place

| Keep a record of | What to write down |
|---|---|
| Travel | Arrival and departure dates, where you were at midnight and supporting bookings. Keep actual dates alongside planned visits. |
| Work | Country, hours, role and employer on each working day. Flag days with more than three hours of UK work. |
| Homes and family | When a home became available or ceased to be a home, where a partner or children lived, and where you stayed on visits. |
| Your starting position | UK residence in the previous three tax years, UK day counts for the previous two, and where this year's income will come from. |
| Changes to income | List your final UK pay, overseas pay, rent, pensions, company payments and any planned sale of shares or property. Keep statements and payment dates. |

## Work out whether you are UK resident for the year

Use HMRC's Statutory Residence Test. At 183 or more UK days you are UK resident. Below that figure, work through the automatic overseas tests, the automatic UK tests and, if neither decides the result, the sufficient ties test. Accommodation, family, work and prior UK days can matter. For recent UK residents, there is also a country tie. The published tables use different thresholds depending on whether you were resident in any of the preceding three tax years. [HMRC’s Statutory Residence Test guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rdr3-statutory-residence-test-srt/guidance-note-for-statutory-residence-test-srt-rdr3).

For example, suppose you plan to spend 65 days back in the UK and were UK resident last year. If no automatic test applies, HMRC's table for 46 to 90 days makes you UK resident at **three or more qualifying ties**. Owning an investment flat alone tells you little: what matters for an accommodation tie is whether the accommodation was available and used under the specific rules. Count the days, then check which ties you actually have. [HMRC’s Statutory Residence Test guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rdr3-statutory-residence-test-srt/guidance-note-for-statutory-residence-test-srt-rdr3).

If the full-year result is UK resident and you leave partway through it, check [split-year treatment](/blog/posts/uk-split-year-treatment-when-leaving). If you run a UK company, use the [founder’s guide to leaving the UK for Paraguay](/blog/posts/uk-to-paraguay-tax-residency) alongside this checklist.

## Tell HMRC through the right route

If you are leaving to live abroad permanently or going abroad to work full-time for at least one full tax year, HMRC asks you to tell it. People outside Self Assessment generally use P85. Include parts 2 and 3 of your P45 if you have them. People already filing returns normally complete the residence pages, SA109, with their departure-year return. HMRC's own online Self Assessment service does not support that departure notification. Use the paper route or compatible commercial software, or ask your accountant to file. Existing filers going abroad to work full-time for a UK-based employer for at least one full tax year should complete P85 as well. [HMRC’s guidance on telling it you are leaving](https://www.gov.uk/tax-right-retire-abroad-return-to-uk).

For a routine 2026/27 return, the standard deadlines are 31 October 2027 on paper and 31 January 2028 online. Check any individual notice and the separate payment deadline. Before you move, check you can access your HMRC account from abroad and arrange a reliable address for correspondence. [GOV.UK’s Self Assessment deadlines](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/deadlines).

## What tax can continue after departure?

- **A UK rental property.** Check the Non-resident Landlord Scheme if you live abroad for six months or more a year. Unless HMRC authorises gross payment, a letting agent, or in some cases your tenant, may have to deduct tax. Approval to receive rent gross still leaves you responsible for declaring taxable rental profit. [UK tax on rent while living abroad](https://www.gov.uk/tax-uk-income-live-abroad/rent).
- **Work and other UK income.** Pay for UK duties, certain pensions and other UK-source income can still require attention. List each income stream and check the rules in both countries. [HMRC’s guide to UK income while living abroad](https://www.gov.uk/tax-uk-income-live-abroad).
- **A planned return.** Certain gains and income received during a temporary absence can be brought into UK tax when you return. Get the dates and transaction history checked before a large disposal or distribution, especially if your return plans change. [HMRC’s guidance on returning to the UK](https://www.gov.uk/tax-return-uk).

As each trip happens, replace the planned dates with actual dates and record any work you did. At the end of the year, send that log to your adviser together with your original assumptions. If Paraguay is your destination, you can prepare your [Paraguay residence documents](/blog/posts/paraguay-residency-uk-documents) in parallel.
